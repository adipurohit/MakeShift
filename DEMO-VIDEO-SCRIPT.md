# MakeShift — 60-Second Demo Video Script

A produced video, not a live stage pitch (that's `PITCH.md`'s 3:30 version — use this one instead for a submitted/social video). Cuts between **SCREEN** (app recording) and **LIVE** (real hands assembling the real Robot Arm). Default example is the Robot Arm build since it's the guaranteed, fully-offline path and the most visually satisfying to film — swap in your actual build if you film something else.

Total runtime: **60 seconds**. Voiceover is deliberately sparse during the live-build montage — let the real footage do the work there; don't talk over it.

## Shot list

| Time | Shot | VO (read exactly as written, or close to it) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | **LIVE** — close-up on the actual scrap: a dead 3D printer, stepper motors, a bent nozzle | "We had two dead 3D printers. Everyone else saw e-waste." | Hands-in-frame, real parts, no app yet. Hook on the problem, not the product. |
| 0:05–0:12 | **SCREEN** — open MakeShift, click Scan Material → Upload a photo instead → pick a photo of the parts | "MakeShift saw a robot arm. Upload a photo—" | Upload is far more reliable to screen-record than a live camera permission prompt and video feed — use it for this beat even if you also demo the live camera elsewhere. Speed up the recording slightly if it drags. |
| 0:12–0:20 | **SCREEN** — Recipe Catalog → select Robot Arm → 2D merge canvas, a part or two snapping together, hologram chips turning from dashed to solid green | "—and it tells you exactly what to build, and shows you where every part goes." | This is the one beat that has to sell the actual product mechanic — don't rush the merge-to-green moment. |
| 0:20–0:38 | **LIVE** — fast-cut montage of real hands actually assembling the robot arm: bolting a joint, threading a belt, wiring the board | *(mostly silent — ambient build sounds: a drill, a belt snapping taut, a click)* "Then you build it for real." | 18 seconds, jump-cut through the real steps. This is the emotional core — give it room. One short VO line only, dropped in partway through, not narrating the whole thing. |
| 0:38–0:48 | **SCREEN** — Build It → assembly steps checked off → Mark Build Complete → confetti/badge. Hard cut to **LIVE**: the finished physical arm, moving | "From scrap to something that actually works." | The screen-to-live cut here is the payoff shot — app says "done," then you immediately show it's real. |
| 0:48–0:60 | **LIVE or SCREEN** — finished arm in frame, logo/wordmark card | "MakeShift. Turn what you have into what you need." | Hold the final frame at least 2 seconds past the VO ending — don't cut on the last word. |

## Full voiceover, read continuously (for recording in one take)

> We had two dead 3D printers. Everyone else saw e-waste.
> MakeShift saw a robot arm. Upload a photo — and it tells you exactly what to build, and shows you where every part goes.
> Then you build it for real.
> From scrap to something that actually works.
> MakeShift. Turn what you have into what you need.

~60 words total. Read it out loud once with a stopwatch before filming — if it's running long, the first line and the live-build line are the two safest to trim; don't cut the final tagline.

## Filming notes

- **Burn in captions.** Social/submission videos get watched muted on a first pass far more often than with sound — don't make the pitch depend on audio.
- **Record the screen capture at normal demo speed, then speed it up 1.3–1.5x in editing** rather than clicking fast on camera — looks smoother and reads as more polished.
- **Shoot more live-build footage than you need.** You only need 18 seconds in the final cut, but real assembly rarely looks great on the first take — overshoot and pick the best bolts-turning, belt-threading, wire-connecting shots in edit.
- **Match the cut, not just the VO, to the beat changes above** — a hard cut from SCREEN to LIVE lands harder than a crossfade for this kind of proof-it's-real moment (especially the 0:38 beat).
- **Light the live-build shots well.** A phone camera with good light beats a nicer camera with bad light — use a window or a desk lamp, not overhead fluorescents if you can help it.
- Do one full dry run of the whole 60 seconds — screen + live — before the real shoot, same as the live-pitch rehearsal in `PITCH.md`.
