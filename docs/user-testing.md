# User testing kit

Goal: watch 3 to 5 people who aren't on the team use MakeShift, and come away with honest quotes and a short list of what confused them. One session takes about 20 minutes.

## Who to recruit

Aim for a mix. At least one person from each group:

- Someone who makes or fixes things (maker space regular, robotics club member, bike mechanic)
- Someone who doesn't (a roommate, a classmate in another major)
- Someone with a drawer of dead electronics they'd like to use

Don't test with teammates or anyone who has seen the pitch.

## Before the session

- Open adipurohit.com/makeshift in a fresh browser profile, so the how-it-works screen appears.
- Ask the person to bring or name 4 to 6 pieces of scrap they actually own. Real inventory beats a script.
- Ask permission to take notes and to quote them by first name, or anonymously if they prefer.

## Tasks (read aloud, then stay quiet)

1. "Here's an app called MakeShift. Take a minute and tell me what you think it does." (30 s, first impression)
2. "Add the scrap you brought." Let them use typing or the camera.
3. "Find something you could build with it." Don't point at AI Ideas or Catalog matches.
4. "Pick one and build it in the app." Note where they get stuck on merging.
5. "Would you actually build this one for real? Why or why not?"

If they're stuck for more than 60 seconds, ask "What are you looking for?" before helping. Note that you helped.

## Questions afterward

- What was the most confusing moment?
- Which suggested build would you really make? Which one was nonsense?
- Would you open this again next time you clean out a drawer? What would make you?
- If you could change one thing, what would it be?

## Turning it into evidence

- Put 2 or 3 exact quotes in PITCH.md, one positive and one critical.
- Count: how many found a build without help, how many said they'd build one for real.
- File each confusing moment as a GitHub issue with the participant's words.

Don't round up. "3 of 4 found a build without help" is stronger than a vague "users loved it".
